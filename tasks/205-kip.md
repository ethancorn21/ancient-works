## Goal
GOAL.md point 4: Johannes Kip's (and Kip & Knyff's) bird's-eye views of country houses and
their formal gardens - the large scans in the Commons categories "Johannes Kip" and
"Britannia Illustrata". Most Britannia Illustrata files on Commons are 300-800 px, too small
to lay over the ground: the six already hand-tied in pipeline/sources.py picked the large
Yale/Rijksmuseum scans, each where something fixed survives to carry a fit. This task finds
the remaining large scans, ties them to catalog sites (existing, or new "lost garden" sites)
with machine-checkable evidence, and stages the rows in its own staging files; 211
consolidates and builds.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Verified before use (docs/lead_kip.md): which Commons categories/pages hold the large
      scans (a count, and the size threshold that makes a scan fit-sized); the six already
      hand-tied (sources.py) are not re-staged - their evidence kind stays "hand-tied QID".
- [ ] Staged rows: files_kip.jsonl for every verified large-scan view that depicts a catalog
      site or a new "lost garden" site - the Hampton Court trap applies (the caption's place,
      not the famous namesake: the Rijksmuseum's "Gezicht op Hampton Court" is
      Herefordshire) - each row with the 200 evidence field (P373 / P180 / caption+coords)
      and a classified row "birdseye" by hand; age recorded on the row (c. 1700s; views are
      exempt from the 150-year rule, recorded anyway).
- [ ] New sites only where the view shows a fixed landmark that can carry a fit (the house,
      the garden's axes, the park); extras_kip.jsonl carries them (kind "garden", the picture
      as the reason to build them - aerial_ok - with lon/lat on the landmark, not the QID's
      municipality point).
- [ ] fit/evidence.py check passes offline on every staged row. At least half of the verified
      large-scan views are staged, or docs/lead_kip.md records why each missing one is not.
- [ ] Test (no network): the staged files join (every row names an existing or staged site;
      every new site has its picture) and the evidence check passes; a golden sample of rows.

## Notes
- A view that shows only a house with nothing fixed left on the ground (no surviving wall,
  tree line, pond edge) is a candidate for the review queue, not a new site: the human
  decides whether to build it from the picture alone.
- "Britannia Illustrata" (1707-09) and Kip's later plates (e.g. in Harris's History of Kent)
  are the same kind of material; one row per picture, not per book.
